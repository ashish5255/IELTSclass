# 1. Introduction to Idioms

## 1.1 What is an idiom?

An **idiom** is a fixed expression or phrase whose overall meaning is
different from the literal meaning of its individual words.

| Idiom | Literal meaning | Actual meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A piece of cake | A portion of cake | Something very easy |
| Break the ice | Break frozen water | Make people feel comfortable |
| Under the weather | Beneath the weather | Feeling unwell |
| Spill the beans | Drop beans | Reveal a secret |
| Hit the nail on the head | Strike a nail accurately | Identify something exactly |
| Once in a blue moon | During a rare blue moon | Very rarely |
| Cost an arm and a leg | Lose limbs | Be extremely expensive |
| Let the cat out of the bag | Release a cat | Reveal a secret accidentally |

## 1.2 Literal vs figurative meaning

**It's raining cats and dogs.** - Literal: Cats and dogs are falling
from the sky. - Figurative: It is raining very heavily.

**He has a heart of gold.** - Literal: His heart is made of gold. -
Figurative: He is extremely kind and generous.

**She broke the ice.** - Literal: She broke a piece of ice. -
Figurative: She made people feel more relaxed.

## 1.3 Why are idioms important?

Idioms help speakers: 1. Express complicated ideas concisely. 2.
Communicate emotions and attitudes naturally. 3. Understand films,
conversations, podcasts and everyday interactions. 4. Recognise cultural
references and figurative language. 5. Make communication more
expressive and engaging.

## 1.4 Idioms and IELTS

Idiomatic language is relevant to IELTS Speaking. Lexical Resource
considers vocabulary range, precision, flexibility and appropriate use.

**Using idioms does not automatically guarantee a higher band score.**

Unnatural: "Education is a piece of cake and technology is raining cats
and dogs."

Natural: "Learning a new language wasn't a piece of cake initially, but
I gradually got the hang of it."

# 2. Understanding the Structure and Meaning of Idioms

## 2.1 Why can't we always understand idioms literally?

Idioms are often based on metaphors, cultural references, historical
expressions and shared experiences.

**Break the ice** describes reducing awkwardness between people who do
not know one another.

Example: "At the beginning of the interview, the interviewer told a joke
to break the ice."

## 2.2 Types of idiom meanings

| Type | Explanation | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Transparent | Meaning is relatively easy to guess | See eye to eye |
| Semi-transparent | Meaning requires interpretation | A blessing in disguise |
| Opaque | Meaning cannot easily be guessed from the words | Kick the bucket |

**Transparent** - See eye to eye --- agree with someone. - Face the
music --- accept the consequences. - Keep an eye on --- watch
carefully. - Get out of hand --- become uncontrollable.

**Semi-transparent** - A blessing in disguise --- something that
initially seems bad but eventually produces a good result. - Burn
bridges --- damage relationships or opportunities permanently. - The tip
of the iceberg --- a small visible part of a much larger problem. - A
storm in a teacup --- excessive concern about a minor issue.

**Opaque** - Kick the bucket --- die. - Let the cat out of the bag ---
reveal a secret. - Bite the bullet --- face a difficult situation
bravely. - Red tape --- excessive bureaucracy.

## 2.3 How idioms create meaning

-   Metaphor: He has a heart of gold.
-   Comparison: She is as cool as a cucumber.
-   Exaggeration: I've told you a million times.
-   Imagery: The news came out of the blue.
-   Cultural association: The ball is in your court.

# 9. Idioms Related to Time and Decisions

-   Once in a blue moon --- very rarely.

-   Time flies --- time passes quickly.
-   Against the clock --- with very little time available.
-   Make up for lost time --- work harder to compensate for lost time.
-   In the long run --- over an extended period.
-   Have time on your hands --- have free time.

-   Sleep on it --- delay a decision until the next day.
-   Weigh the pros and cons --- consider advantages and disadvantages.
-   Be at a crossroads --- face an important choice.
-   Take the plunge --- make a bold decision.
-   Play it safe --- avoid unnecessary risks.
-   Go with your gut --- follow your instinct.
-   Make up your mind --- decide.
-   A tough call --- a difficult decision.
-   The ball is in your court --- it is your turn to act.
-   Leave no stone unturned --- try every possible option.

Examples: - I'll sleep on it before deciding. - We should weigh the pros
and cons. - I'm at a crossroads in my career. - I've made my offer; the

# 13. Commonly Confused Idioms

## A blessing in disguise vs a silver lining

-   **A blessing in disguise:** A seemingly bad event that eventually
    turns out to be good.
-   **A silver lining:** The positive aspect of an otherwise difficult
    situation.

Examples: - Losing that job was a blessing in disguise because it
encouraged me to start a business. - The silver lining was that I became
more independent.

## Once in a blue moon vs every now and then

-   **Once in a blue moon:** Very rarely.
-   **Every now and then:** Occasionally.

Examples: - I eat there once in a blue moon. - I go hiking every now and
then.

## At the eleventh hour vs in the nick of time

-   **At the eleventh hour:** At the last possible moment, often
    suggesting delay.
-   **In the nick of time:** Just before it is too late.

Examples: - He submitted the application at the eleventh hour. - The
ambulance arrived in the nick of time.

## Out of the blue vs once in a blue moon

-   **Out of the blue:** Unexpectedly.
-   **Once in a blue moon:** Very rarely.

## Hit the roof vs hit the nail on the head

-   **Hit the roof:** Become extremely angry.
-   **Hit the nail on the head:** Identify something exactly.

## A hard nut to crack vs a tough cookie

-   **A hard nut to crack:** A difficult problem or person to understand
    or deal with.
-   **A tough cookie:** A strong person who is difficult to defeat.

## Get cold feet vs have butterflies in your stomach

-   **Get cold feet:** Become nervous and hesitate before doing
    something.
-   **Have butterflies in your stomach:** Feel nervous excitement, often
    before an event.

## Between a rock and a hard place vs at a crossroads

-   **Between a rock and a hard place:** Trapped between two difficult
    options.
-   **At a crossroads:** Facing an important decision about direction or
    future.

# 14. Idioms vs Phrasal Verbs vs Proverbs vs Collocations

## 14.1 Idioms

Expressions whose overall meaning is not necessarily predictable from
their individual words. - A piece of cake --- something easy. - Spill
the beans --- reveal a secret. - Under the weather --- feeling unwell.

## 14.2 Phrasal verbs

A verb combined with an adverb particle, a preposition, or both. - Give
up --- quit. - Look after --- take care of. - Put off --- postpone. -
Run into --- meet unexpectedly.

## 14.3 Proverbs

Short, commonly known sayings expressing advice, wisdom or a general
truth.

| Proverb | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Actions speak louder than words. | What people do matters more than what they say. |
| Practice makes perfect. | Repeated practice improves ability. |
| Better safe than sorry. | It is wiser to avoid risks than regret them. |
| Don't judge a book by its cover. | Do not judge by appearance alone. |
| Where there's a will, there's a way. | Determination helps people find solutions. |
| Two heads are better than one. | Cooperation can produce better ideas. |
| The early bird catches the worm. | Acting early can provide an advantage. |
| Honesty is the best policy. | Being truthful is generally best. |

## 14.4 Collocations

Natural combinations of words that frequently occur together. - Make a
decision. - Take responsibility. - Heavy rain. - Strong argument. - Deep
sleep. - Pay attention. - Highly recommended. - Raise awareness.

## 14.5 Comparison

| Category | Main feature | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Idiom | Figurative or conventional expression | A piece of cake |
| Phrasal verb | Verb + particle | Give up |
| Proverb | Saying expressing advice or wisdom | Practice makes perfect |
| Collocation | Words that naturally go together | Make a decision |

Categories can overlap. Some idioms contain phrasal verbs, and
expressions may be analysed differently depending on the linguistic
framework. The table shows their typical learning distinction.

# 15. Grammar and Correct Usage of Idioms

## 15.1 Fixed wording

Some idioms have a relatively fixed form. Changing a word may make the
expression incorrect or unnatural.

Correct: - It costs an arm and a leg. - She spilled the beans. - He hit
the nail on the head.

Incorrect: - It costs an arm and a foot. - She poured the beans. - He
hit the nail on its head.

## 15.2 Articles in idioms

Articles are often part of the expression.

Correct: - A piece of cake. - A blessing in disguise. - The last
straw. - A storm in a teacup. - The tip of the iceberg.

Example: "Getting that rejection was a blessing in disguise."

## 15.3 Pronouns and possessives

Some idioms change according to the person or object: - Keep your chin
up. - He kept his chin up. - She kept her chin up. - Get something off
my chest. - Get something off your chest. - He needed to get something
off his chest. - Have your hands full. - She has her hands full.

## 15.4 Tense changes

| Base expression | Past form | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Spill the beans | Spilled the beans | She spilled the beans yesterday. |
| Break the ice | Broke the ice | He broke the ice with a joke. |
| Bite the bullet | Bit the bullet | I bit the bullet and apologised. |
| Hit the nail on the head | Hit the nail on the head | You hit the nail on the head. |
| Burn the midnight oil | Burned the midnight oil | They burned the midnight oil. |
| Let the cat out of the bag | Let the cat out of the bag | He let the cat out of the bag. |

## 15.5 Idioms in negative sentences

-   I wouldn't miss it for the world.
-   Don't make a mountain out of a molehill.
-   I don't want to jump to conclusions.
-   She didn't bat an eyelid.
-   He never takes no for an answer.

## 15.6 Idioms in questions

-   Are you pulling my leg?
-   Do you think it's worth a shot?
-   Have you made up your mind?
-   Why are you sitting on the fence?
-   Should we call it a day?

## 15.7 Common grammatical mistakes

| Incorrect | Correct | Explanation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| He is in the cloud nine. | He is on cloud nine. | Correct preposition is *on*. |
| It is raining cats and dogses. | It is raining cats and dogs. | Fixed expression. |
| She has a heart of golds. | She has a heart of gold. | Do not pluralise *gold*. |
| He spilled a bean. | He spilled the beans. | Conventional expression uses plural *beans*. |
| I am under weather. | I am under the weather. | Article is required. |
| He hit on the nail's head. | He hit the nail on the head. | Keep the word order. |
| We are in same boat. | We are in the same boat. | Article is required. |
| I got cold foot. | I got cold feet. | Idiom uses plural *feet*. |

# 16. Formal vs Informal Idiomatic Expressions

Idioms are common in conversation, but not all are suitable for every
situation. Some are informal, humorous or strongly conversational.
Others are widely accepted in professional communication.

## 16.1 Informal idioms and neutral alternatives

| Idiom | Meaning | More neutral alternative |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A piece of cake | Very easy | Straightforward |
| Cost an arm and a leg | Very expensive | Extremely costly |
| Spill the beans | Reveal a secret | Reveal confidential information |
| Hit the roof | Become very angry | Become extremely angry |
| Pull someone's leg | Tease someone | Joke with someone |
| Get cold feet | Become hesitant | Lose confidence |
| Bite the bullet | Face something difficult | Confront the situation |
| Call it a day | Stop working | Conclude the work |
| A long shot | An unlikely possibility | An unlikely outcome |
| In hot water | In trouble | In difficulty |
| Make ends meet | Manage financially | Cover basic expenses |
| The last straw | Final unacceptable event | Final triggering incident |
| Out of the blue | Unexpectedly | Without prior warning |
| Keep your fingers crossed | Hope for success | Hope for a positive outcome |
| Get the ball rolling | Start a process | Initiate the process |

## 16.2 Idioms in IELTS Speaking

Idioms can be useful when they are relevant, accurate, natural and
supported by clear explanations.

Example:

Question: Do you enjoy learning new skills?

Answer: "Yes, definitely. I think learning a new skill is always
worthwhile. It may not be a piece of cake at first, but once you get the
hang of it, the process becomes much more enjoyable."

## 16.3 Idioms in IELTS Writing

IELTS Writing Task 1 and Task 2 require an appropriate academic or
semi-formal register. Many conversational idioms are better avoided in
formal essays.

Conversational: "Technology is a double-edged sword."

More academic: "Technology has both significant benefits and potential
disadvantages."

Conversational: "Governments need to bite the bullet."

More academic: "Governments need to make difficult but necessary
decisions."

Some established expressions, such as *in the long run* or *the tip of
the iceberg*, may be appropriate depending on context. The aim is not to
eliminate every figurative expression, but to maintain precision and
register.

# 17. IELTS Speaking Part 1: Questions and Model Answers

Part 1 answers should be direct, personal and relatively concise. Idioms
should fit naturally rather than appear in every sentence.

## Hobbies and free time

**Q1. What do you usually do in your free time?**

I usually watch films, listen to music or spend time with friends. After
a hectic day, I just like to take it easy and forget about work for a
while.

*Idiom: take it easy.*

**Q2. Do you enjoy learning new skills?**

Yes, I do. Learning something new can be challenging at first, but once
you get the hang of it, it becomes quite enjoyable. I think it also
keeps the mind active.

*Idiom: get the hang of it.*

**Q3. Do you prefer spending your free time alone or with others?**

It depends on my mood. Sometimes I enjoy my own company, especially when
I need to recharge. At other times, I like meeting friends and having a
good time.

*Expression: have a good time.*

## Friends

**Q4. Do you have a large group of friends?**

Not really. I prefer having a small circle of close friends rather than
knowing lots of people. I value relationships where we can support each
other through thick and thin.

*Idiom: through thick and thin.*

**Q5. What do you usually do with your friends?**

We usually go out for food, watch films or simply sit and talk. We can
talk for hours because we are on the same wavelength.

*Idiom: on the same wavelength.*

## Technology

**Q6. Do you use technology every day?**

Absolutely. Technology is part and parcel of my daily routine. I use it
for work, communication, learning and entertainment.

*Expression: part and parcel of.*

**Q7. Do you think people depend too much on technology?**

In some cases, yes. Technology is a double-edged sword. It makes life
easier, but excessive use can affect concentration and face-to-face
communication.

*Idiom: a double-edged sword.*

## Food

**Q8. Do you enjoy cooking?**

Yes, although I'm not an expert. I can prepare a few basic dishes, and I
sometimes experiment with recipes. Some turn out well, while others are
a bit hit and miss.

*Idiom: hit and miss.*

**Q9. Do you often eat out?**

Not very often. I prefer home-cooked food, but I eat out once in a
while, especially when I meet friends.

*Expression: once in a while.*

## Travel

**Q10. Do you like travelling?**

Definitely. Travelling gives me a chance to get away from my routine and
explore new places. I also think it broadens your horizons.

*Idiom: broaden your horizons.*

**Q11. Do you prefer travelling with friends or family?**

I enjoy both, but travelling with friends can be particularly exciting
because we share similar interests. We usually have a great time
together.

*Expression: have a great time.*

## Work and study

**Q12. Do you enjoy your work or studies?**

Yes, I do. It keeps me busy and gives me opportunities to learn. There
are challenging days, of course, but I believe hard work pays off in the
long run.

*Idioms: hard work pays off; in the long run.*

**Q13. Do you prefer working alone or in a team?**

I prefer a combination of both. Teamwork is useful because two heads are
better than one, but I also need time to focus independently.

*Proverb: two heads are better than one.*

## Shopping

**Q14. Do you enjoy shopping?**

I enjoy it when I actually need something. However, I am not someone who
spends money at the drop of a hat. I usually compare prices before
buying anything expensive.

*Idiom: at the drop of a hat.*

**Q15. Have you ever bought something expensive?**

Yes, I once bought a laptop that cost an arm and a leg. However, it was
worth every penny because I use it almost every day.

*Idioms: cost an arm and a leg; worth every penny.*

## Childhood and memories

**Q16. What do you remember most about your childhood?**

I remember spending a lot of time outdoors with my friends. We didn't
need expensive toys to have fun; we could make a game out of almost
anything. Those memories still bring a smile to my face.

*Expression: bring a smile to my face.*

**Q17. Did you enjoy going to school?**

Yes, I did. I enjoyed meeting my friends and participating in different
activities. I wasn't always enthusiastic about examinations, though, and
I had to hit the books quite often.

*Idiom: hit the books.*

# 18. IELTS Speaking Part 2: Cue Cards and Model Answers

## Cue Card 1: Describe a person who has influenced you.

You should say: - Who the person is - How you know this person - What
qualities this person has - And explain how this person has influenced
you

### Model answer

One person who has had a significant influence on my life is my father.
I have always admired him because of his discipline, patience and
ability to handle difficult situations.

He has taught me that success does not happen overnight and that
consistency is more important than short bursts of motivation. He often
reminds me that hard work pays off in the long run.

What I admire most is that he has a heart of gold. He is always willing
to help people, even when he has very little to gain. He has also stood
by me during difficult periods, which has given me confidence.

I would say that he has shaped the way I approach my personal and
professional life. I hope I can follow in his footsteps and make him
proud.

**Idioms:** have a heart of gold; hard work pays off; in the long run;
stand by someone; follow in someone's footsteps.

## Cue Card 2: Describe a difficult situation you successfully dealt with.

You should say: - What the situation was - When it happened - What you
did - And explain how you felt afterwards

### Model answer

A difficult situation I remember clearly was when I had several
important assignments to complete within a very short period.

Initially, I felt as though I was between a rock and a hard place
because I had too much work and very little time. I was also worried
that I might disappoint people who were depending on me.

Instead of panicking, I decided to bite the bullet and organise
everything. I divided the work into smaller tasks and focused on the
most urgent ones first. I had to burn the midnight oil on a couple of
occasions, but eventually I completed everything.

Looking back, I realised that the experience was a blessing in disguise.
It taught me how to manage pressure and prioritise responsibilities. I
also learned that when the going gets tough, the tough get going.

**Idioms:** between a rock and a hard place; bite the bullet; burn the
midnight oil; a blessing in disguise; when the going gets tough, the
tough get going.

## Cue Card 3: Describe a memorable journey you had.

You should say: - Where you went - Who you travelled with - What you
did - And explain why the journey was memorable

### Model answer

One of the most memorable journeys I have ever had was a trip to a
beautiful hill station with a group of friends.

We set off early in the morning because we wanted to make the most of
the day. The weather was perfect, and the scenery was breathtaking. We
stopped at several places along the way to take photographs.

When we reached our destination, we explored the local area and tried
some traditional food. We were all in high spirits, and the atmosphere
was fantastic. Even though a few things did not go according to plan, we
decided to play it by ear.

What made the journey special was the time we spent together. We
laughed, shared stories and enjoyed being away from our usual routines.
I still look back on that trip with a smile because it was one of those
experiences that money can't buy.

**Idioms:** make the most of; in high spirits; play it by ear; look back
on; money can't buy.

## Cue Card 4: Describe a skill you would like to learn.

You should say: - What the skill is - Why you want to learn it - How you
would learn it - And explain how it would benefit you

### Model answer

A skill I would really like to improve is public speaking. Although I
can communicate comfortably in everyday situations, I sometimes find it
challenging to express complex ideas confidently in front of a large
audience.

I believe effective communication can open doors in both personal and
professional life. At the moment, I am considering joining a course and
practising regularly. It may be a steep learning curve initially, but I
am prepared to put in the effort.

I would also like to watch experienced speakers and learn from them. I
know that practice makes perfect, so I would try to take every
opportunity to speak in front of an audience.

Ultimately, I believe this skill would help me think on my feet, make a
good impression and take on greater responsibilities in the future.

**Idioms:** open doors; a steep learning curve; practice makes perfect;
think on your feet; make a good impression.

## Cue Card 5: Describe a time when you helped someone.

You should say: - Who the person was - What problem they had - How you
helped - And explain how you felt about it

### Model answer

I remember helping a close friend who was struggling with an important
academic assignment. He was finding it difficult to organise his ideas
and was worried that he would not finish the work on time.

I offered to sit with him and go through the requirements. We discussed
the main points and tried to think outside the box to find a suitable
approach. I also encouraged him not to lose heart because there was
still enough time to complete the work.

Eventually, he managed to finish the assignment and submit it before the
deadline. He was over the moon with the result, and I was genuinely
happy for him.

The experience reminded me that a little support can make a world of
difference. Sometimes, simply being there for someone is enough to help
them get through a difficult period.

**Idioms:** think outside the box; lose heart; over the moon; make a
world of difference; be there for someone.

## Cue Card 6: Describe a time when you changed your opinion about something.

You should say: - What you used to think - What changed your opinion -
How you feel about it now - And explain why your opinion changed

### Model answer

I used to believe that working from home was less productive than
working in a traditional office. I thought people would become
distracted and find it difficult to maintain a professional routine.

However, my opinion gradually changed after I observed how effectively
some people managed their work remotely. I began reading about the
subject and realised that I had only been looking at one side of the
coin.

I came to understand that productivity depends more on individual
discipline and the nature of the job than on the physical workplace. In
fact, remote working can make a world of difference for people who have
long commutes or family responsibilities.

I still believe office-based work has its advantages, but I no longer
dismiss remote working as an inferior option. I have learned not to
judge a book by its cover.

**Idioms:** one side of the coin; make a world of difference; judge a
book by its cover.

## Cue Card 7: Describe a time when you were extremely happy.

You should say: - When it happened - What happened - Who was with you -
And explain why you felt so happy

### Model answer

One of the happiest moments in my life was when I received an important
academic result. I had worked hard for several months, and I was anxious
while waiting for the announcement.

When I finally saw the result, I was over the moon. My family was
equally excited, and everyone was grinning from ear to ear. It felt as
though all the effort had finally paid off.

What made the moment even more special was being able to share it with
my family. They had supported me through thick and thin, so the
achievement belonged to them as well.

I still remember that day very clearly. It reminded me that patience and
hard work can lead to wonderful outcomes.

**Idioms:** over the moon; grin from ear to ear; pay off; through thick
and thin.

## Cue Card 8: Describe a time when you made an important decision.

You should say: - What the decision was - Why you made it - Whether it
was difficult - And explain how you felt afterwards

### Model answer

An important decision I made was choosing a particular career path. At
the time, I was at a crossroads because I had several options, and each
had its own advantages and disadvantages.

I did not want to rush into anything, so I took some time to weigh the
pros and cons. I also spoke to people with experience in the field and
tried to get a clearer picture of what the future might look like.

Eventually, I decided to take the plunge. It was not an easy decision,
but I felt that I needed to step out of my comfort zone if I wanted to
grow.

Looking back, I am glad I made that choice. It taught me that sometimes
you have to take a leap of faith and trust your judgement.

**Idioms:** at a crossroads; weigh the pros and cons; take the plunge;
step out of your comfort zone; take a leap of faith.

# 19. IELTS Speaking Part 3: Questions and Model Answers

Part 3 requires more analytical answers. Idioms can help express
opinions, evaluate situations and explain consequences, but clarity
should remain the priority.

## Education

**Q1. Why do some students perform better than others?**

I think several factors come into play, including motivation, learning
habits and the support students receive. Some students are naturally
disciplined, while others may struggle to stay focused. At the end of
the day, consistent effort and a supportive learning environment make a
significant difference.

*Expressions: come into play; at the end of the day.*

**Q2. Should schools encourage students to take up extracurricular
activities?**

Definitely. Academic performance is important, but it is not the be-all
and end-all of education. Extracurricular activities help students
develop teamwork, communication and leadership skills. They also give
students an opportunity to discover their interests.

*Expression: the be-all and end-all.*

**Q3. Is practical education more important than theoretical
education?**

I would say both are equally important. Theory provides the foundation,
while practical experience allows students to put their knowledge to
use. One without the other can leave learners at a disadvantage.

*Expression: at a disadvantage.*

## Technology

**Q4. Do people rely too much on technology?**

In many ways, yes. Technology is a double-edged sword. It makes life
more convenient, but excessive dependence can reduce people's ability to
solve simple problems independently. The key is to strike a balance.

*Idioms: a double-edged sword; strike a balance.*

**Q5. How has technology changed communication?**

Technology has made communication faster and more accessible. People can
stay in touch regardless of distance. However, there is another side to
the coin: online communication can sometimes lack the warmth and depth
of face-to-face interaction.

*Expression: the other side of the coin.*

**Q6. Will artificial intelligence replace human workers?**

I think AI will replace some repetitive tasks, but it is unlikely to
make human workers unnecessary across every industry. Rather than
viewing it as the end of the road, people should see it as a reason to
adapt and develop new skills.

*Expression: the end of the road.*

## Environment

**Q7. Why do people sometimes ignore environmental problems?**

One reason is that environmental damage can seem distant or difficult to
understand. People may focus on immediate concerns and overlook the
long-term consequences. However, what we see today may only be the tip
of the iceberg.

*Expression: the tip of the iceberg.*

**Q8. Should governments take stronger action against pollution?**

Absolutely. Governments need to take the bull by the horns and enforce
environmental regulations. Awareness campaigns alone are not enough;
there must be practical action and accountability.

*Expression: take the bull by the horns.*

**Q9. Can individual actions make a difference to the environment?**

Yes, although one person's contribution may seem like a drop in the
ocean, collective action can have a significant impact. Small changes in
daily habits can create a ripple effect across communities.

*Expressions: a drop in the ocean; a ripple effect.*

## Work and employment

**Q10. What makes someone successful in their career?**

I think success depends on consistency, adaptability and the willingness
to learn. Talent may open doors, but people need to work hard and make
the most of opportunities. They also need to be willing to go the extra
mile.

*Expressions: open doors; make the most of; go the extra mile.*

**Q11. Is job satisfaction more important than salary?**

It depends on a person's circumstances. A good salary is important
because people need to make ends meet, but job satisfaction also affects
motivation and mental well-being. Ideally, people should find a balance
between the two.

*Expressions: make ends meet; find a balance.*

**Q12. Why do some employees experience burnout?**

Many employees are expected to work long hours and handle unrealistic
workloads. When people are constantly under pressure and have little
time to recharge, they can eventually reach breaking point. Employers
need to recognise that productivity should not come at the expense of
well-being.

*Expressions: reach breaking point; come at the expense of.*

## Family and society

**Q13. Are family relationships as strong as they were in the past?**

I think family relationships have changed rather than simply become
weaker. People may live farther apart because of work and education, but
technology helps them stay connected. What matters is whether family
members make time for one another.

**Q14. Why is it important to support older people?**

Older people have contributed a great deal to their families and
communities. Supporting them is not simply an act of kindness; it is
also a way of giving something back. A society should not turn a blind
eye to their needs.

*Expressions: give something back; turn a blind eye.*

**Q15. How can communities become more connected?**

Communities become stronger when people take an active interest in one
another. Local events, volunteering and shared projects can bring people
together. A small gesture can go a long way in building trust.

*Expression: go a long way.*

## Money and consumer behaviour

**Q16. Why do some people find it difficult to save money?**

Many people struggle because their spending habits are influenced by
advertising and social pressure. They may try to keep up with the
Joneses and purchase things they do not really need. Financial education
can help people make more sensible decisions.

*Expression: keep up with the Joneses.*

**Q17. Do people spend too much money on luxury goods?**

Some people do, particularly when they want to keep up appearances or
impress others. However, buying something expensive is not necessarily
irresponsible if a person can afford it. The important thing is not to
live beyond one's means.

*Expressions: keep up appearances; live beyond one's means.*

**Q18. Should children be taught about money management?**

Absolutely. Children should learn that money doesn't grow on trees. They
need to understand saving, budgeting and responsible spending from an
early age. These lessons can make a world of difference later in life.

*Expressions: money doesn't grow on trees; make a world of difference.*

## Travel and tourism

**Q19. How can tourism benefit local communities?**

Tourism can create jobs, support small businesses and bring investment
into local areas. It can also put lesser-known destinations on the map.
However, authorities need to ensure that economic benefits do not come
at the expense of local culture and the environment.

*Expressions: put something on the map; come at the expense of.*

**Q20. What problems can mass tourism cause?**

Mass tourism can put pressure on infrastructure, increase living costs
and damage natural attractions. In some places, the visible problems may
only be the tip of the iceberg because the long-term effects are much
harder to measure.

*Expression: the tip of the iceberg.*

**Q21. Should governments invest more in domestic tourism?**

Yes. Domestic tourism can encourage people to explore their own country
and support local economies. It can also help spread the benefits of
tourism beyond the usual hotspots. In the long run, this may create a
more balanced tourism industry.

*Expressions: in the long run; spread the benefits.*

## Final learning principle

**Learn idioms as complete expressions, not as isolated words.**

For every idiom, learn: 1. Its figurative meaning. 2. Its literal image,
if relevant. 3. Its grammatical structure. 4. Its level of formality. 5.
The situations in which it is naturally used. 6. A realistic example
sentence. 7. Whether it is suitable for IELTS Speaking or formal
writing.

The goal is not to insert as many idioms as possible into a
conversation. The goal is to express ideas clearly, naturally and
appropriately.

**A good command of idioms comes from understanding when to use
them---and when not to.**
