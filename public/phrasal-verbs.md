# 1. Introduction

## What is a phrasal verb?

A **phrasal verb** is a combination of a verb and one or more particles
(an adverb, a preposition, or both). Together, they create a meaning
that may be different from the original verb.

| Verb | Particle | Phrasal verb | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| give | up | give up | quit or stop doing something |
| look | after | look after | take care of |
| put | off | put off | postpone |
| find | out | find out | discover information |
| run | into | run into | meet unexpectedly |

### Compare the meanings

-   **Look:** She looked at the painting.
-   **Look after:** She looks after her younger brother.
-   **Look into:** The police are looking into the incident.
-   **Look up:** I looked up the word in a dictionary.

The verb *look* remains the same, but the particle changes the meaning.

## Why are phrasal verbs important?

Phrasal verbs are common in everyday English, informal conversations,
films, podcasts, workplace communication, and spoken English
examinations.

They help speakers: - Express ideas naturally and concisely. -
Understand native and fluent English speakers. - Describe experiences
and situations more precisely. - Demonstrate a flexible range of
vocabulary in IELTS Speaking.

**Important:** Using a phrasal verb does not automatically make an
answer advanced. Accuracy, context, and naturalness matter more than
complexity.

# 2. Understanding the Structure

## The basic formula

A phrasal verb generally follows one of these patterns:

### Verb + adverb particle

-   wake up
-   sit down
-   give up
-   carry on
-   break down

### Verb + preposition

-   look after
-   rely on
-   deal with
-   listen to
-   believe in

### Verb + adverb + preposition

-   look forward to
-   get along with
-   put up with
-   come up with
-   run out of

## Understanding particles

Particles can sometimes give clues about meaning. However, these are
patterns, not fixed rules. The meaning of a phrasal verb must ultimately
be learned in context.

| Particle | Common idea | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| up | completion, increase, or preparation | use up, speed up, set up |
| down | reduction, control, or stopping | slow down, cut down, break down |
| out | discovery, disappearance, or distribution | find out, run out, hand out |
| off | separation, cancellation, or departure | take off, call off, set off |
| on | continuation or connection | carry on, put on, get on |
| away | distance or continuous action | go away, throw away, work away |
| back | return or response | come back, call back, take back |
| over | review or repetition | go over, think over, take over |

### Examples in context

-   The company has **set up** a new office.
-   We need to **cut down on** plastic waste.
-   I eventually **found out** what had happened.
-   The meeting was **called off** because of bad weather.
-   She decided to **carry on** with her studies.

# 3. Types of Phrasal Verbs

## 3.1 Transitive phrasal verbs

A transitive phrasal verb requires an object.

**Pattern:** Verb + particle + object

Examples:

-   I **turned off** the television.
-   She **picked up** the package.
-   They **called off** the meeting.
-   We **looked into** the problem.

The object receives the action.

## 3.2 Intransitive phrasal verbs

An intransitive phrasal verb does not require an object.

**Pattern:** Verb + particle

Examples:

-   The car **broke down**.
-   I usually **wake up** early.
-   The children **grew up** in Pokhara.
-   The plane **took off** on time.

## 3.3 Three-word phrasal verbs

These contain a verb followed by two particles.

**Pattern:** Verb + particle + preposition

Examples:

| Phrasal verb | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| look forward to | feel excited about something in the future | I look forward to meeting you. |
| get along with | have a good relationship | I get along with my colleagues. |
| put up with | tolerate | I cannot put up with constant noise. |
| come up with | think of an idea | She came up with a solution. |
| run out of | have no more of something | We have run out of time. |
| look down on | consider someone inferior | Nobody should look down on others. |
| get away with | escape punishment | He cannot get away with cheating. |
| catch up with | reach the same level or exchange news | I need to catch up with my friends. |

# 4. Separable and Inseparable Phrasal Verbs

## 4.1 Separable phrasal verbs

With a separable phrasal verb, the object can appear either after the
particle or between the verb and particle.

-   Turn off the light.
-   Turn the light off.

Both sentences are correct.

### Pronoun rule

When the object is a pronoun, it must usually go between the verb and
the particle.

-   Turn **it** off. ✓
-   Turn off **it**. ✗

More examples:

| With a noun | With a pronoun |
| --- | --- |
| Pick up the book. | Pick it up. |
| Put on your jacket. | Put it on. |
| Write down the answer. | Write it down. |
| Throw away the rubbish. | Throw it away. |
| Hand in the assignment. | Hand it in. |

## 4.2 Inseparable phrasal verbs

The verb and particle must stay together.

-   I look after my younger brother.
-   I look after him.

Not: I look him after.

Examples:

| Phrasal verb | Correct example |
| --- | --- |
| look after | She looks after her grandparents. |
| run into | I ran into an old friend. |
| deal with | We need to deal with this issue. |
| look into | The committee will look into the matter. |
| get over | It took me weeks to get over the flu. |
| come across | I came across an interesting article. |

## 4.3 Three-word verbs

Three-word phrasal verbs are generally inseparable.

-   I look forward to the holiday.
-   She gets along with her classmates.
-   We have to put up with the noise.

# 6. Confusing and Similar Phrasal Verbs

## Give up vs give in

-   **Give up:** stop trying or quit.
-   **Give in:** stop resisting or accept something after pressure.

Examples: - I will never give up on my dreams. - After a long argument,
he finally gave in.

## Put off vs call off

-   **Put off:** postpone until a later time.
-   **Call off:** cancel completely.

Examples: - We put off the meeting until Friday. - They called off the
match because of the rain.

## Find out vs figure out vs work out

-   **Find out:** discover information.
-   **Figure out:** understand or solve something.
-   **Work out:** calculate, solve, or reach a solution.

Examples: - I found out that the shop was closed. - I cannot figure out
how this machine works. - We worked out the final cost.

## Look up vs look up to

-   **Look up:** search for information.
-   **Look up to:** admire someone.

Examples: - Look up the word in a dictionary. - I have always looked up
to my grandfather.

## Look into vs look over

-   **Look into:** investigate.
-   **Look over:** examine or review quickly.

Examples: - The authorities are looking into the complaint. - Could you
look over my essay?

## Take up vs take on

-   **Take up:** start an activity, hobby, or subject.
-   **Take on:** accept work, a role, or responsibility.

Examples: - I took up swimming last year. - She took on additional
responsibilities.

## Break down vs break up vs break out

-   **Break down:** stop functioning; lose emotional control; analyse
    into parts.
-   **Break up:** end a relationship; separate into pieces.
-   **Break out:** begin suddenly, often referring to conflict, fire, or
    disease.

Examples: - My car broke down on the way home. - They broke up after
five years together. - A fire broke out in the building.

## Bring up vs bring about vs bring out

-   **Bring up:** mention a topic; raise a child.
-   **Bring about:** cause a change.
-   **Bring out:** publish, release, or make a quality noticeable.

Examples: - She brought up an interesting point. - Technology has
brought about major changes. - The experience brought out his
confidence.

## Turn up vs turn out vs turn down

-   **Turn up:** arrive; increase volume or intensity.
-   **Turn out:** prove to be; result in a particular way.
-   **Turn down:** reject; reduce volume or intensity.

Examples: - He turned up late. - The event turned out to be
successful. - She turned down the job offer.

## Bring back vs take back

-   **Bring back:** return something to a place; revive a memory.
-   **Take back:** return something to its original owner; withdraw a
    statement.

Examples: - Please bring back my book tomorrow. - I take back what I
said.

## Get by vs get through

-   **Get by:** manage with limited resources.
-   **Get through:** complete a difficult period or task; make contact
    by phone.

Examples: - Students often get by on a tight budget. - I managed to get
through the examination. - I could not get through to the office.

## Fall behind vs fall out vs fall apart

-   **Fall behind:** fail to maintain progress.
-   **Fall out:** have an argument.
-   **Fall apart:** break into pieces or become emotionally overwhelmed.

Examples: - I fell behind with my coursework. - The two friends fell out
over money. - The old building is falling apart.

# 8. Grammar and Advanced Usage

## 8.1 Phrasal verbs in different tenses

Phrasal verbs follow the normal tense rules of the main verb.

| Tense | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Present simple | I give up easily. |
| Present continuous | I am looking into the issue. |
| Past simple | She called off the meeting. |
| Past continuous | They were setting up the equipment. |
| Present perfect | I have taken up swimming. |
| Past perfect | He had already handed in the assignment. |
| Future simple | We will work out a solution. |
| Modal | You should cut down on sugar. |

## 8.2 Irregular verb forms

| Base form | Past simple | Past participle | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| give up | gave up | given up | She has given up smoking. |
| take off | took off | taken off | The plane has taken off. |
| break down | broke down | broken down | The machine has broken down. |
| find out | found out | found out | I found out the truth. |
| come across | came across | come across | I have come across this phrase before. |
| grow up | grew up | grown up | He grew up in Nepal. |
| run into | ran into | run into | I ran into an old friend. |
| write down | wrote down | written down | I have written down the answer. |
| throw away | threw away | thrown away | They threw away the old files. |
| put up | put up | put up | They have put up a new sign. |

## 8.3 Passive voice

Some transitive phrasal verbs can be used in the passive voice.

**Active:** The company called off the meeting.

**Passive:** The meeting was called off.

More examples:

-   The proposal was turned down.
-   The new policy will be brought in next year.
-   The issue is being looked into.
-   The results have been written down.

## 8.4 Phrasal verbs followed by -ing

Some phrasal verbs are followed by a gerund (-ing form).

-   I look forward to **meeting** you.
-   She ended up **spending** more than expected.
-   He gave up **smoking**.
-   They carried on **working**.
-   I cannot put up with **waiting** for hours.

**Common mistake:** In *look forward to*, the word *to* is a
preposition, so it is followed by a noun or gerund, not the base verb.

-   I look forward to meeting you. ✓
-   I look forward to meet you. ✗

## 8.5 Phrasal verbs with multiple meanings

### Take off

1.  A plane leaves the ground: The plane took off.
2.  Remove clothing: Take off your shoes.
3.  Become successful quickly: Her career took off.

### Pick up

1.  Collect someone: I will pick you up.
2.  Lift something: Pick up the bag.
3.  Learn informally: Children pick up languages quickly.
4.  Improve: Business is beginning to pick up.

### Make up

1.  Invent a story: He made up an excuse.
2.  Reconcile: They made up after the argument.
3.  Constitute a proportion: Women make up half the workforce.
4.  Apply cosmetics: She made herself up.

### Go off

1.  An alarm rings: The alarm went off.
2.  Food becomes bad: The milk has gone off.
3.  Explode: The bomb went off.
4.  Stop liking something: I have gone off coffee.

### Break down

1.  Stop working: The car broke down.
2.  Become emotionally overwhelmed: She broke down in tears.
3.  Divide into parts: Let's break down the problem.

### Turn out

1.  Prove to be: It turned out well.
2.  Attend an event: A large crowd turned out.
3.  Produce: The factory turns out thousands of products.

Phrasal verbs are not automatically informal. Some are suitable in
academic and professional contexts, while others are conversational.
Choose vocabulary according to the situation.

| set up | establish |

| point out | highlight |

| go down | decrease |

| bring about | cause |
| deal with | address / handle |
| think over | consider |
| get rid of | eliminate |

## IELTS Speaking vs IELTS Writing

### Speaking

Natural phrasal verbs can make spoken answers sound fluent and
conversational.

Example:

"I used to spend a lot of time playing video games, but I gradually
**cut down on** them because I wanted to focus on my studies."

### Academic Writing

In formal essays, prioritise precision, clarity, and an appropriate
academic register.

Less formal: "Governments should **crack down on** companies that
pollute rivers."

More formal: "Governments should **impose stricter penalties on**
companies that pollute rivers."

**IELTS tip:** Lexical Resource is not a competition to use the largest
number of difficult words. Accuracy, flexibility, collocation, and
appropriateness are essential.

# 10. Phrasal Verbs vs Idioms

## Phrasal verbs

A verb combined with a particle to create a particular meaning.

-   give up
-   look after
-   put off
-   find out

## Phrasal-prepositional verbs

A verb followed by an adverb and a preposition.

-   look forward to
-   put up with
-   get along with
-   come up with

## Idioms

Expressions whose overall meanings are often not predictable from the
individual words.

-   a piece of cake --- something very easy
-   once in a blue moon --- very rarely
-   break the ice --- make people feel comfortable
-   hit the nail on the head --- describe something exactly
-   under the weather --- feeling unwell

### Compare

-   **Give up:** a phrasal verb meaning to quit.
-   **Put up with:** a phrasal-prepositional verb meaning to tolerate.
-   **A piece of cake:** an idiom meaning something very easy.

# 11. IELTS Speaking Part 1

The following sample answers demonstrate how phrasal verbs can be used
naturally in short, personal responses.

## Topic: Hobbies and free time

**Q1. What do you usually do in your free time?**

I usually **wind down** by watching a film or listening to music. If I
have enough time, I also **hang out** with my friends or go for a walk.
It helps me take my mind off work.

*Phrasal verbs: wind down, hang out, take my mind off.*

**Q2. Have you taken up any new hobbies recently?**

Yes, I have recently **taken up** photography. I am still learning the
basics, but I enjoy **trying out** different techniques and taking
pictures of landscapes.

*Phrasal verbs: take up, try out.*

## Topic: Friends

**Q3. Do you often meet your friends?**

Yes, although we are all quite busy, we try to **catch up** whenever we
get the chance. Sometimes we **head over** to a café or simply spend
time chatting.

*Phrasal verbs: catch up, head over.*

**Q4. What qualities do you value in a friend?**

I value honesty and reliability. I want friends I can **count on**,
especially when things do not **work out** as planned.

*Phrasal verbs: count on, work out.*

## Topic: Technology

**Q5. How often do you use your phone?**

I use it throughout the day, mainly to **keep up with** work and stay in
touch with people. However, I try to **cut down on** unnecessary screen
time.

*Phrasal verbs: keep up with, cut down on.*

**Q6. What would you do if your phone broke down?**

I would probably take it to a repair shop first. If they could not
**sort it out**, I would consider buying a new one.

*Phrasal verbs: break down, sort out.*

## Topic: Food

**Q7. Do you enjoy cooking?**

Yes, I do. I enjoy **trying out** new recipes, although I sometimes
**mess up** the measurements. Fortunately, most of the time the food
turns out fine.

*Phrasal verbs: try out, mess up, turn out.*

**Q8. Do you often eat out?**

Not very often. I prefer home-cooked meals, but I occasionally **eat
out** with friends, particularly at weekends.

*Phrasal verb: eat out.*

## Topic: Travel

**Q9. Do you like travelling?**

Absolutely. Travelling gives me an opportunity to **get away** from my
routine and explore unfamiliar places. I also enjoy **looking around**
local markets and trying regional food.

*Phrasal verbs: get away, look around.*

**Q10. Do you prefer travelling alone or with others?**

I prefer travelling with friends because we can share experiences and
**look out for** one another. However, travelling alone can also help
people become more independent.

*Phrasal verb: look out for.*

## Topic: Work and study

**Q11. What do you like most about your work or studies?**

I enjoy learning new things and finding ways to **put them into
practice**. I also like working with people because I can learn from
their experiences.

*Expression: put into practice.*

**Q12. Is there anything you would like to change about your daily
routine?**

I would like to **cut down on** the time I spend on social media and
**set aside** more time for reading and exercise.

*Phrasal verbs: cut down on, set aside.*

## Topic: Shopping

**Q13. Do you enjoy shopping?**

I enjoy shopping when I need something specific. I usually **shop
around** before buying expensive items because I want to get good value
for money.

*Phrasal verb: shop around.*

**Q14. Do you make quick purchasing decisions?**

Not usually. I prefer to **think things over** and compare different
options before spending a significant amount of money.

*Phrasal verb: think over.*

## Topic: Childhood and memories

**Q15. What is one thing you remember from your childhood?**

I often **look back on** my school days with fondness. They **bring
back** memories of spending time with friends and taking part in school
events.

*Phrasal verbs: look back on, bring back.*

**Q16. Did you enjoy reading as a child?**

Yes, although I did not read regularly. I sometimes **picked up**
storybooks from the school library, especially during holidays.

*Phrasal verb: pick up.*

# 12. IELTS Speaking Part 2

Each cue card includes a model response and a list of useful phrasal
verbs.

## Cue Card 1: Describe a person who has influenced you.

You should say: - Who the person is - How you know this person - What
qualities this person has - And explain how this person has influenced
you

### Model answer

One person who has had a significant influence on my life is my father.
I have always **looked up to** him because of his discipline, patience,
and ability to handle difficult situations.

He has always encouraged me to take responsibility for my decisions
rather than depend on others. Whenever I faced a challenge, he would
remind me not to **give up** too easily and to keep working towards my
goals.

What I admire most is that he never tries to impose his opinions on me.
Instead, he explains different perspectives and allows me to make my own
decisions. He has also **stood by** me during difficult periods, which
has given me a great deal of confidence.

I would say that he has influenced the way I approach both my personal
and professional life. I hope I can **live up to** the values he has
taught me.

**Useful phrasal verbs:** look up to, give up, stand by, live up to,
work towards.

## Cue Card 2: Describe a difficult situation you successfully dealt with.

You should say: - What the situation was - When it happened - What you
did - And explain how you felt afterwards

### Model answer

A difficult situation I remember clearly was when I had several
important assignments to complete within a very short period.

Initially, I felt overwhelmed because the work had started to **pile
up**, and I was worried that I would not be able to meet the deadlines.
However, instead of panicking, I decided to **sort out** my priorities
and divide the work into smaller tasks.

I created a schedule and focused on completing one task at a time.
Whenever I felt distracted, I reminded myself of the deadlines and tried
to stay focused. It was exhausting, but I managed to **get through** the
situation without compromising the quality of my work.

Looking back, I realised that the experience taught me how important
time management and self-discipline are. It also showed me that even
stressful situations can be handled if we approach them systematically.

**Useful phrasal verbs:** pile up, sort out, get through, look back,
work out.

## Cue Card 3: Describe a memorable journey you had.

You should say: - Where you went - Who you travelled with - What you
did - And explain why the journey was memorable

### Model answer

One of the most memorable journeys I have ever had was a trip to a
beautiful hill station with a group of friends.

We **set off** early in the morning because we wanted to make the most
of the day. The journey itself was fascinating, as we passed through
small villages, green hills, and winding roads.

When we reached our destination, we spent some time exploring the area.
We also **came across** a small local restaurant where we tried
traditional food. It was not part of our original plan, but it turned
out to be one of the highlights of the trip.

What made the journey special was the time we spent together. We took
photographs, shared stories, and enjoyed being away from our usual
routines. I still **look back on** that trip as one of the happiest
experiences of my life.

**Useful phrasal verbs:** set off, come across, turn out, look back on,
get away.

## Cue Card 4: Describe a skill you would like to learn.

You should say: - What the skill is - Why you want to learn it - How you
would learn it - And explain how it would benefit you

### Model answer

A skill I would really like to improve is public speaking. Although I
can communicate comfortably in everyday situations, I sometimes find it
challenging to express complex ideas confidently in front of a large
audience.

I have been thinking about taking up a structured course, but I have not
managed to **get around to** it yet. If I decide to pursue it seriously,
I would probably **sign up for** a course and practise regularly.

I would also like to **brush up on** my presentation skills by watching
experienced speakers and analysing how they organise their ideas.

I believe this skill would be useful in both my personal and
professional life. It would help me communicate more effectively, share
ideas confidently, and perhaps **take on** leadership responsibilities
in the future.

**Useful phrasal verbs:** get around to, sign up for, brush up on, take
on, think about.

## Cue Card 5: Describe a time when you helped someone.

You should say: - Who the person was - What problem they had - How you
helped - And explain how you felt about it

### Model answer

I remember helping a close friend who was struggling with an important
academic assignment. He was finding it difficult to organise his ideas
and was worried that he would not finish the work on time.

I offered to sit with him and go through the requirements. We first
**looked over** the instructions and then **came up with** a simple
plan. I helped him organise the main points and suggested a few
resources that he could use.

I did not complete the assignment for him, of course, but I tried to
guide him through the process. I also encouraged him to **reach out to**
his teacher whenever he was unsure about something.

Eventually, he managed to finish the work and submit it on time. I felt
genuinely happy because I knew that my support had helped him become
more confident.

**Useful phrasal verbs:** look over, come up with, reach out to, work
through, help out.

## Cue Card 6: Describe a time when you changed your opinion about something.

You should say: - What you used to think - What changed your opinion -
How you feel about it now - And explain why your opinion changed

### Model answer

I used to believe that working from home was less productive than
working in a traditional office. I thought people would become
distracted and find it difficult to maintain a professional routine.

However, my opinion gradually changed after I started observing how
effectively some people managed their work remotely. I began to **look
into** the advantages of remote working and came across several examples
of people who were able to maintain a healthy work-life balance.

I also realised that productivity depends more on individual discipline
and the nature of the job than on the physical workplace.

Eventually, I **came around to** the idea that remote working can be
effective when organisations have appropriate systems in place. I still
believe that office-based work has its advantages, but I no longer
dismiss remote working as an inferior option.

**Useful phrasal verbs:** look into, come across, come around to, put in
place, work out.

# 13. IELTS Speaking Part 3

These questions require more developed answers. The examples show how
phrasal verbs can be used to explain causes, consequences, comparisons,
and opinions.

## Education

**Q1. Why do some students perform better than others?**

I think several factors come into play, including motivation, learning
habits, and the support students receive. Some students are naturally
more disciplined and manage to **keep up with** their coursework,
whereas others may **fall behind** because they struggle with time
management.

**Q2. Should schools encourage students to take up extracurricular
activities?**

Definitely. Extracurricular activities allow students to **take up**
interests beyond academic subjects. They also help students **build on**
their existing abilities, develop teamwork, and become more confident.

**Q3. How has technology changed education?**

Technology has **brought about** significant changes in education.
Students can now access online resources, attend virtual classes, and
**look up** information within seconds. However, schools need to ensure
that students do not become overly dependent on digital tools.

## Technology

**Q4. Do you think people rely too much on technology?**

In many ways, yes. People have become so accustomed to digital devices
that they sometimes struggle to **get by** without them. While
technology makes life more convenient, excessive dependence can affect
communication and problem-solving skills.

**Q5. What problems can technology create in the workplace?**

One major problem is that employees may find it difficult to **keep up
with** constant technological changes. In addition, technical systems
can **break down**, causing delays and disrupting normal operations.

**Q6. Will artificial intelligence take over many jobs?**

I think AI will certainly **take over** some repetitive tasks, but it
may also create new employment opportunities. Rather than trying to
resist technological change, workers should **brush up on** their
digital skills and prepare for new roles.

## Environment

**Q7. Why do some people fail to protect the environment?**

A lack of awareness and convenience are two major reasons. Some people
simply **put off** environmentally friendly habits because they do not
see immediate benefits. Others may **brush aside** environmental
concerns because they consider them less important than economic issues.

**Q8. What can governments do to reduce pollution?**

Governments should **crack down on** industries that violate
environmental regulations. They should also **phase out** highly
polluting technologies and invest in cleaner alternatives.

**Q9. Is individual action enough to solve environmental problems?**

Individual action is important, but it is not sufficient on its own.
Governments and businesses must also **step up** their efforts. For
example, companies should **cut down on** unnecessary packaging, while
governments should introduce effective waste-management systems.

## Work and employment

**Q10. Why do people change jobs?**

People change jobs for several reasons. Some want to **move up** in
their careers, while others are looking for better salaries or a
healthier work environment. Some employees also leave because they can
no longer **put up with** workplace pressure.

**Q11. What makes someone successful in their career?**

I believe success depends on consistency, adaptability, and the
willingness to learn. People who continually **build on** their skills
and **take on** new responsibilities are more likely to progress
professionally.

**Q12. Should employees follow their passion or choose a stable job?**

Ideally, people should find a balance between the two. Passion can
provide motivation, but financial stability is also important. Before
making a decision, individuals should **weigh up** the advantages and
disadvantages rather than make an impulsive choice.

## Family and society

**Q13. Do family relationships change as people grow older?**

Yes, they often do. People become more independent as they grow up, and
family members may gradually **grow apart** because of work or other
commitments. However, regular communication can help them **stay in
touch** and maintain close relationships.

**Q14. Why is it important to support older people?**

Older people have contributed greatly to their families and communities.
Society should not **leave them behind**. Governments and families need
to **look after** their well-being and ensure that they can live with
dignity.

**Q15. How can communities become more connected?**

Communities can become more connected when people **reach out to** their
neighbours and participate in local activities. Community events can
also help people **get to know** one another and develop a stronger
sense of belonging.

## Money and consumer behaviour

**Q16. Why do some people find it difficult to save money?**

Many people struggle to save because they spend too much on
non-essential items. They may also **get carried away** by discounts and
advertisements. Developing a budget and setting money aside regularly
can help.

**Q17. Do you think people spend too much money on luxury goods?**

Some people do, particularly when they want to **show off** their
financial status. However, others see luxury purchases as a personal
reward. I think the main issue is whether people can afford them without
getting into debt.

**Q18. Should children be taught about money management?**

Absolutely. Children should learn how to **put aside** money, plan their
spending, and understand the difference between needs and wants. These
habits can help them become financially responsible adults.

## Travel and tourism

**Q19. How can tourism benefit local communities?**

Tourism can **bring in** revenue, create employment, and support local
businesses. It can also encourage communities to preserve their cultural
heritage. However, local authorities must ensure that tourism does not
damage the environment.

**Q20. What problems can mass tourism cause?**

Mass tourism can **push up** property prices and put pressure on local
infrastructure. If authorities do not manage visitor numbers properly,
popular destinations may become overcrowded and polluted.

**Q21. Should governments invest more in domestic tourism?**

Yes, because domestic tourism can help distribute economic benefits
across different regions. Governments should **draw up** development
plans, improve transport links, and **carry out** promotional campaigns
to encourage people to explore their own countries.

## Final learning principle

**Learn phrasal verbs in context, not as isolated vocabulary items.**

For every new phrasal verb, learn: 1. Its meaning. 2. Its grammatical
pattern. 3. Whether it is separable or inseparable. 4. Its common
collocations. 5. One natural example from everyday life. 6. One example
connected to an IELTS Speaking topic.

The goal is not to use as many phrasal verbs as possible. The goal is to
use the right expression, in the right context, with the right grammar.
